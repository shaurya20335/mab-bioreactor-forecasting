# Alignment with the B.Tech report

**Completed extension:** the final implementation includes a multivariate
LSTM, MLP, and ridge comparison on 88 simulated batches, saved inference, and
a final report. See [final_report.md](final_report.md) for current results and
[README.md](../README.md) for commands. Ridge was selected on validation data;
its unseen-batch RMSE is 0.00521 mg/L. The detailed univariate numbers below
document the initial report-architecture baseline, not the final benchmark.

## Research objective

The primary project brief is [project_proposal.pdf](project_proposal.pdf),
*Data Driven Modeling of monoclonal antibody (mAb) production in bioreactor
using LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) Neural Networks*.

The intended work is to generate bioreactor time series, learn their sequential
behavior with an LSTM, and evaluate mAb concentration forecasts. Simulation is
the data-generation stage. Accurate prediction and eventual monitoring are
research objectives; the report does not specify a completed control or
optimization implementation.

## Method choices needed to make the report reproducible

The PDF does not provide its original training source, saved model, scaler,
random seed, or complete split procedure. The implementation therefore follows
its architecture without claiming to reproduce the plotted model weights or
reported error values.

- 5,001 samples at one-minute intervals, from the corrected simulator.
- Training targets: 20–3,199 minutes; validation targets: 3,200–3,999 minutes;
  test targets: 4,000–5,000 minutes. These are 64/16/20 percent row boundaries,
  with the first 20 training rows used as history.
- A 20-sample input at this cadence spans 20 minutes; the one-step target is
  one minute ahead. The recursive future example extends to 6,000 minutes.
- Min-max scaling fitted only on training observations. Held-out values may
  exceed the scaled training range; they are not clipped.
- Adam, learning rate 0.001, batch size 64, up to 50 epochs, early stopping
  after 10 epochs without a new best validation loss, and seed 42 on CPU.
  These settings are explicit implementation choices where the PDF is silent.
- Validation chooses the checkpoint. Rolling one-step test scores and a
  separate recursive test forecast are evaluated after that selection.
- Persistence predicts the last observation. Linear drift extrapolates the
  difference between the last two observations. Both use only information
  available at the same forecast origin as the LSTM.
- MAE/RMSE use mg/L; MSE uses (mg/L)². Training loss is separately labeled
  scaled MSE. Data hashes, versions, settings, and the scaler are saved.

## Initial univariate findings

These historical findings document the initial experiment. Its generated files
are excluded from the repository; source and tests remain in `baselines/` and
`tests/`, and the experiment can be regenerated with the command above.

The first deterministic experiment selected epoch 1 and stopped after epoch 11.
This is a first baseline result; test performance was not used to tune it.

| Method | Rolling one-step test RMSE (mg/L) | Recursive test RMSE (mg/L) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Report-architecture LSTM | 23.4015 | 31.2326 |
| Persistence | 0.0575995 | 32.3036 |
| Linear drift | 0.0000131415 | 3.06651 |

The LSTM has not demonstrated an advantage over these simple baselines.
The extremely small one-step drift error reflects a smooth, noise-free,
minute-sampled synthetic trajectory; it is not evidence of industrial accuracy.
The recursive LSTM forecast flattens, and its unobserved future forecast drops
from the final supplied concentration. These are model failures visible in the
exported plots, not a biological prediction of reactor behavior.

The available training region ends at about 68.08 mg/L, while the test segment
extends to about 161.62 mg/L. This experiment therefore tests extrapolation
beyond the training concentration range. One synthetic trajectory also provides
no independent evidence of generalization across reactor batches.

## Completed Research Work

The new multivariate forecasting framework (in the `forecasting/` directory) successfully addresses steps 2-4:
2. **Multi-batch generation**: Multiple distinct batches spanning relevant operating conditions are now generated and kept strictly separate for training, validation, and testing.
3. **Multivariate comparison**: The final benchmark compares persistence, drift, quadratic continuation, ridge regression, MLPs, and LSTMs on the same multivariate task. Ridge regression is selected on validation batches. The historical univariate LSTM is retained separately; its different task prevents direct score comparisons.
4. **Horizons and robustness**: The framework evaluates various prediction leads, conformal prediction intervals (for coverage and uncertainty), and includes a predefined measurement noise sensitivity stress-test.
